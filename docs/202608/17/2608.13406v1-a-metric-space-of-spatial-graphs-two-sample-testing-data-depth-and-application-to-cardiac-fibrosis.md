## 摘要
心脏纤维化会降低电导率，是心律失常的主要原因。心律失常波通常围绕非传导性纤维化斑块旋转，因此这些斑块（心肌内纤维化组织的空间孤立区域）的几何形状和拓扑结构在心律失常动力学中起着重要作用。尽管具有临床相关性，这些结构仍未被充分理解。我们利用受心脏纤维化影响的人体心脏的组织病理学图像来解决这一开放问题。每个斑块通过骨架化表示为空间图，其中节点嵌入为欧几里得空间中的点，边编码底层组织的几何属性。本工作的核心方法贡献是引入空间图空间，这是一个配备旋转不变的融合格罗莫夫-瓦瑟斯坦度量的度量空间，能够比较具有不同节点和边数量的空间图。在此基础上，我们进行了空间图的分布级统计检验和深度度量。为了解释空间图样本分布，我们引入了DepthPlot，一种用于度量空间中深度度量的新颖可视化工具。应用我们的方法比较患者以及斑块在心室内的空间位置，我们发现纤维化纹理表现出强烈的患者特异性特征，而一些心脏显示出显著的几何相似性，可能反映共同的病理突变或其他未知因素。通过定量深度度量，我们通过中心和外围空间图来表征检验结果，证明所提出的框架对纤维化纹理表征产生了统计学和临床上都有意义的见解。

## Abstract
Cardiac fibrosis reduces electrical conductivity and is a leading cause of arrhythmia. Arrhythmic waves typically rotate around non-conducting fibrotic patches, so the geometry and topology of these patches (spatially isolated regions of fibrotic tissue within the heart muscle) play an important role in arrhythmia dynamics. Despite their clinical relevance, these structures remain poorly understood. We address this open problem using histopathological images of human hearts affected by cardiac fibrosis. Each patch is represented as a spatial graph via skeletonization, where nodes are embedded as points in Euclidean space and edges encode geometric properties of the underlying tissue. The core methodological contribution of this work is the introduction of spatial graph space, a metric space equipped with a rotation-invariant Fused Gromov Wasserstein metric that enables comparison of spatial graphs with differing numbers of nodes and edges. Building on this, we perform a distribution-level statistical testing and depth measures for spatial graphs. To enable the interpretation of the spatial graph sample distribution, we introduce DepthPlot, a novel visualization tool for depth measures in metric spaces. Applying our methodology to compare patients and the spatial position of patches within the ventricles, we find that fibrotic textures exhibit strong patient-specific features, while some hearts display notable geometric similarities, potentially reflecting shared pathological mutations or other unknown factors. Through quantitative depth measures, we characterize test outcomes via central and peripheral spatial graphs, demonstrating that the proposed framework yields statistically and clinically meaningful insights into fibrotic texture characterization.
